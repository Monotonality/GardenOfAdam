If you look at the current competitive job landscape for students across fields like Computer Science, Data Science, and Analytics, a hard truth becomes obvious: **a degree alone is no longer enough.**

Every year, thousands of students graduate with that piece of paper, expecting the corporate world to welcome them with open arms. Too often, they find a market that demands much more. To thrive, students need three things that traditional coursework rarely provides all at once:

1. **Exposure to cutting-edge tech:** An active understanding of the tools and technologies currently being developed and deployed in the real world, rather than outdated curricula.
2. **Practical application:** Hands-on experience translating theoretical classroom concepts into real application or research projects that form a compelling portfolio.
3. **Industry network:** Meaningful connections with researchers, professors, and industry professionals who understand where the field is actually heading.

My dream for **[A.A.I.R. Labs](https://www.aairlabs.org/about)** (Applied AI Research Labs at UT Dallas) is simple: a student should be able to walk into this organization in their first year of college, and by the time they graduate, possess a deep understanding of cutting-edge technology, a robust portfolio of completed projects, and a rich network of real industry relationships and mentorship stories.

## The spark: how AAIR Labs started

The idea came to me half-formed, of course—as most ideas do when they first show up.

After finishing a class with Professor Judd Bradbury, he invited me to be an independent contributor to his research project simply because he liked how attentive and quick I was in class. That single opportunity opened my eyes to two major realities:

1. I realized how surprisingly little I knew about the bleeding-edge technologies shaping our field—a dangerous blind spot given how rapidly AI evolves.
2. Professors face a massive operational friction point. They rarely have a structured, reliable talent pool to easily source dedicated student researchers, assistants, or survey participants.

I realized that AAIR Labs could solve both problems simultaneously. It could act as a bridge, connecting motivated students hungry for real-world application with professors who need reliable, high-performing research talent.

## More than just a club: building a community of builders

Once the core foundation was set, I wanted to expand the vision.

Before I had taken [daRSVP](/writing/what-is-darsvp/) to [Actriant's Research Lab](https://www.actriant.com/), I was already developing it purely out of personal interest—but I realized I knew many other students pursuing rigorous projects in their own silos, much in the same way. For instance, I knew peers building advanced systems like facial recognition software paired with large-scale social media data analytics to track shifting political alignments, as well as a friend researching how artificial intelligence has affected global business and disseminated across industries over the past decade.

Talented builders were out there working in silos. I wanted a place where builders—students like them and myself—could gather to:

- Showcase what they are actively creating.
- Learn directly from each other's code and frameworks.
- Collaborate on difficult technical problems.
- Secure mentorship and guidance from experienced professors who know how to scale academic and technical rigor.

## The vision ahead

I want AAIR Labs to be something that gives back to the members. I want it to be a place to ignite the flame of curiosity and fan it with capable peers who are excited and passionate to build the next big thing. It's built to ensure that no student has to graduate wondering how to cross the chasm between theory and practice.

If you're a student who wants to build real things, dive into active research affecting the field you'll graduate into, and surround yourself with builders, [we built AAIR Labs for you](https://www.aairlabs.org/about).
